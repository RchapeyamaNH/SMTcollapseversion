Recommendation

Audit and comparison: Form 1 vs. Form 2

Form 1 shows every CoO order field to every CoO user, every time. Form 2 shows only the fields that apply to the order type being placed. The difference is the difference between a form that's always generically complete and a form that's always correctly scoped.

Less to think about

Staff and Other orders aren't cluttered with Incentive-only fields, and vice versa. Every field on screen is relevant to the order being placed, so there's nothing to mentally filter out.

Faster to complete

Incentive orders need 20 fields instead of 93. For CoO staff processing many orders a day, that's less scrolling and fewer decisions on every single one.

Lower error risk

Fields that don't belong to an order type simply aren't shown, so there's no chance of someone filling in or misreading a field that has nothing to do with their order.

Matches how the business works

The field sets come directly from the order-type columns the business already maintains, so the form reflects real operational rules instead of approximating them with one generic layout.

Nothing mandatory disappears

Every Required and CoO-editable field is preserved for every order type. Scoping removes noise, not substance.

Scales to the rest of SMT

The same approach, filter by order type, rank by field importance, can generate right-sized forms for Pre-Event, Post-Event, and School Give-Back next.

Recommendation: ship Form 2

Form 2 is the form to bring to the client. The data itself shows the three order types genuinely need different fields, the clearest example being Ship-To address, which is system-generated for Incentive orders (pulled from the school's CRM record) but must be entered by hand for Staff and Other orders (no CRM record exists for an individual recipient). One static form can't represent that correctly for every type at once. A dynamic one can, and it does so using the business's own rules, not a guess.

Methodology. Source: "CoO Order Fields" tab, SEOT Detailed Requirements & Decision Log. Form 1 uses every field in rows A3–A45 and A49–A98 (row 2 and row 48 are section headers, not fields).

Form 2 is built per order type from the matching column (Staff, Incentive, or Other) plus the shared "All Order Types" rows. Fields are ranked Required > CoO-editable > Optional > View-only > shared context > System-generated, then kept in that priority order, in the sequence they appear in the source sheet, until the target count is reached (40 for Staff, 20 for Incentive, 50 for Other). Required and CoO-editable fields are never cut by this process for any order type.

Field behavior is read directly from each field's own letter code, per the legend embedded in the sheet: R = Required, O = Optional, D = CoO can edit, V = View only, S = System generated.

Wizard: the same 93 fields, one step at a time

All 93 fields from Form 1, broken into four steps with Back and Next navigation instead of one long scroll.

Why this falls short: a wizard doesn't remove a single field that doesn't apply to an order, it just spreads the same 93-field form across more clicks and more page transitions. CoO staff moving quickly through routine orders now wait on multiple steps instead of one scannable screen, can't see the full scope of the order up front, and pay a real cost in back-and-forth if they need to fix something on an earlier step. It trades one long problem for a slower version of the same problem.
